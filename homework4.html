Photomontage

Final Export

Layers Panel

My photomontage centers around the fire hose cabinet, symbolizing how violence was ingrained in everyday public spaces, even within institutions meant to be safe. I layered archival images of the Little Rock students entering school, illustrating how ordinary actions like attending class were perceived as conflicts. The cabinet represents the pressure used to enforce segregation, while the students embody movement, courage, and the sacrifices made simply by showing up.

I used layer masking to blend the images, keeping the cabinet as the main focus while the historical photo stands out as a memory and context. I applied blending modes — Soft Light, Overlay, Color Dodge, and Hard Mix — and adjusted the opacity to create a poster-like collage. I also took the mural and added a Color Halftone filter from the Pixelate menu to give it that grunge look to match the feeling. The mural texture adds a modern layer, showing how history can be hidden or softened, even if the harmful structures are still there.
